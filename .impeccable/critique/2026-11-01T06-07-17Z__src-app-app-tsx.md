## Priority issues

### P2 — Keep palette search editable while navigating results
Evidence: CommandPalette.tsx handleKeyDown focuses result buttons. Live: after typing reset and pressing ArrowDown, focus moved to Reset zoom. Typing more left the query unchanged at reset.
Why: Alex commonly scans results and immediately refines a query. Requiring a pointer or reverse Tab interrupts that loop; Sam must track an additional focus transition.
Fix: Keep focus in the search input with an active result index and aria-activedescendant, or explicitly route printable typing back to search without swallowing text. Keep unavailable reasons and Enter safety intact.
Suggested command: $impeccable optimize.

### P2 — Let Tab leave menus in the expected direction
Evidence: shared/hooks/use-menu-placement.ts navigateMenu treats Tab and Escape identically, prevents default and returns focus to the trigger. This source behavior applies to MenuButton, ContextMenu and commit action menus. Escape return was live verified; the Tab consequence is source-derived.
Why: Keyboard users pressing Tab expect forward movement. Returning to the trigger makes another Tab necessary, and Shift+Tab loses its expected direction as well.
Fix: Close on Tab and move focus to the adjacent logical control in the requested direction; retain explicit trigger return for Escape. Verify both Tab directions with a menu opened by keyboard.
Suggested command: $impeccable harden.

### P2 — Prioritize the current Reset preview over recovery history
Evidence: ResetDialog.tsx renders the Restore a reset backup section ahead of preview facts and impact text. In the live expanded 960×700 review, the empty recovery card consumed substantial space before What this reset will change; the acknowledgement fell below the scroll fold.
Why: The modal's primary job is reviewing the proposed operation. An empty backup section competes with the explanation needed for an informed decision, especially in a short window.
Fix: Place recovery in a collapsed secondary disclosure after the preview or behind a clearly labeled recovery entry. Keep the operation's target, consequences and confirmation in the first reading sequence. Preserve the persistent footer.
Suggested command: $impeccable distill.

## Emotional journey
The entry experience is calm and the palette feels lighter than its command inventory. Available actions make experimentation low-pressure. Unavailable reasons turn a dead end into an explanation. Reset creates the appropriate moment of caution: branch/mode/commit counts and a plain-language impact panel explain the stakes, and the fixed Cancel action stays reachable. The emotional valley is attention cost rather than danger: the empty recovery card appears before the requested impact. Success and post-operation recovery were not exercised, so the review cannot judge the final peak/end of an executed Git operation.
